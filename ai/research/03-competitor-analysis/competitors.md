# Competitor / Reference Analysis — sprayfoamscience.com

Reference set for content style: authoritative science/education content in this niche, not local
contractor lead-gen pages. All are real, well-established resources in the spray foam industry.

## Spray Polyurethane Foam Alliance (SPFA) — spraypolyurethane.org
The industry trade association. Content is technical, standards-and-code-driven, aimed at both
installers and building professionals. Trust signal: association credibility, code/standards
citations (ICC, ASTM). Depth: high, but written for an industry audience, not consumer-friendly.
**Gap we can fill:** SPFA content assumes technical literacy; there's room for a plain-English
science explainer that doesn't sacrifice accuracy.

## SprayFoam.com / SprayFoamMagazine.com
Trade-publication style: news, contractor directories, product announcements, some technical
explainers. Consumer-facing content is thinner and less structured as an evergreen knowledge base.
**Gap:** not built as a topic-cluster education hub; content is publication-dated, not organized
around the questions a homeowner or specifier is actually asking.

## Manufacturer technical resources (Icynene, Demilec, Lapolla, BASF SPF, Owens Corning)
Each manufacturer publishes technical data sheets and some educational content, but it is
inherently product-promotional — R-value claims and comparisons are framed to favor their own
formulation. Trust signal: manufacturer authority on their own product's spec sheet.
**Gap:** no manufacturer site will honestly cover the "why spray foam gets a bad reputation"
cluster (bad installs, moisture-trapping failures, resale/insurance friction) because it's
adjacent to their own product liability. That's the open lane for an independent, CCA-backed site.

## Building science community references (e.g. buildingscience.com-style technical writing)
Where genuinely rigorous building-science treatment of vapor barriers, moisture physics, and
assembly design exists. Written for architects/engineers — dense, not organized as consumer-facing
topic pages.
**Gap:** the rigor is real but the audience is wrong for most of our search demand (homeowners and
DIY researchers, per the PAA data — see keywords.json).

## Common patterns across all references
- Trust built on citations to real standards (IECC, ASTM, ICC) and manufacturer spec sheets, not
  star ratings or customer testimonials.
- Long-form, multi-section technical pages rather than short marketing copy.
- Comparison tables (open-cell vs closed-cell, R-value by product) are a recurring, effective format.
- None of the references directly and honestly address the "spray foam is now a red flag" consumer
  narrative found in live PAA data — this is the differentiation gap sprayfoamscience.com fills.
