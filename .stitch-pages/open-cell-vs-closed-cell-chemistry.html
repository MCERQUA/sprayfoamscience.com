Chemistry

Open Cell vs Closed Cell Chemistry

A technical deep-dive into the molecular differences, thermal performance, and moisture management properties of the two primary types of spray polyurethane foam.

The Chemistry of Polyurethane Foam

At its core, spray polyurethane foam (SPF) is created through an exothermic chemical reaction between two primary components: an isocyanate (the 'A' side) and a polyol resin blend (the 'B' side). When these liquid components are mixed at high pressure and temperature in the spray gun, they undergo a rapid polymerization process. The distinction between open and closed cell varieties arises primarily from differences in the blowing agents used and the specific chemical formulation of the polyol side, which dictates how the polymer matrix forms and expands.

Cell Structure and Density Differences

The physical structure of the foam dictates its mechanical and thermal properties. In open cell foam, the blowing agent creates gas bubbles that burst during the expansion phase. The result is a structure of interconnected, hollow cavities—or cells—that share walls. This gives open cell foam its characteristic sponge-like feel, low density (typically around 0.5 lbs/cubic foot), and excellent sound-absorbing capabilities.

Conversely, closed cell foam utilizes blowing agents that remain trapped within the individual cells during polymerization. The cell walls remain intact, forming a rigid structure composed of millions of tiny, discrete, gas-filled spheres. This intact structure results in a much higher density (typically 2.0 lbs/cubic foot or greater), increased structural rigidity, and superior thermal resistance due to the low thermal conductivity of the trapped gas.

Where Each Type Performs Best

Application selection depends heavily on climate zone, building assembly, and specific performance goals. Open cell foam is often preferred for interior cavity fill, such as in roof decks or unvented attics in milder climates, where its expansion capabilities allow it to thoroughly seal complex framing details and its lower cost is advantageous. It is also an excellent choice for interior sound attenuation.

Closed cell foam is the standard for applications requiring high thermal resistance in a thin profile, or where moisture resistance is critical. Its rigidity adds racking strength to walls, making it ideal for exterior continuous insulation, below-grade applications (foundation walls), and in assemblies prone to high moisture drive where a class II vapor retarder is required.

Vapor Permeability Differences

Understanding vapor permanence is arguably the most critical aspect of specifying SPF. Open cell foam is highly vapor permeable (typically >10 perms at 3 inches), meaning moisture vapor can pass through it relatively easily. In many assemblies, this requires the addition of a separate vapor retarder to prevent interstitial condensation during cold weather.

Closed cell foam, due to its intact cell structure, acts as a vapor retarder. At thicknesses of 1.5 to 2.0 inches, most closed cell foams achieve a Class II vapor retarder rating (1.0 perm or less). This integrated moisture management capability simplifies many wall and roof assemblies by eliminating the need for a separate interior vapor barrier.
