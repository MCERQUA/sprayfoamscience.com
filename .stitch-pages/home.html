An Objective Approach to Building Science

Spray foam insulation is a site-manufactured chemical product. Its performance relies entirely on the precise execution of chemistry under variable environmental conditions. When applied correctly, it offers unparalleled air sealing and thermal resistance. When applied incorrectly, it can lead to catastrophic moisture issues, persistent odors, and compromised structural integrity.

Our methodology is rooted in objective, referee-style building science. We do not engage in fear-mongering regarding chemical outgassing, nor do we act as an uncritical marketing arm for manufacturers. We dissect failure modes through the lens of root-cause engineering—examining off-ratio mixing, improper substrate preparation, and inappropriate moisture physics design.

By understanding the physics of heat, air, and moisture flow, architects and builders can specify foam correctly, and homeowners can make informed decisions grounded in reality, not rhetoric.

Technical Clarifications

Does spray foam need an intumescent coating for fire code? expand_more

It depends strictly on the application location and the specific product testing (ICC-ES Evaluation Reports). When installed in an unoccupied attic or crawlspace, many foams only require an ignition barrier (or have passed Appendix X testing without one). However, if left exposed in an occupied space, the IBC and IRC generally require a 15-minute thermal barrier, typically achieved via 1/2-inch gypsum board or a specifically tested intumescent coating applied at the correct mil thickness.

What causes the "fish odor" sometimes reported after installation? expand_more

Persistent odors are almost universally a symptom of an incomplete chemical reaction, commonly referred to as "off-ratio" spraying. This occurs when the A-side (isocyanate) and B-side (polyol resin containing amine catalysts) are not mixed at exactly a 1:1 ratio, or if barrel temperatures and pressures are not maintained. The amine catalysts, which have an inherent fish-like odor, remain unreacted and off-gas into the environment. Proper equipment calibration and applicator training prevent this failure mode.

Can I use open-cell foam below grade? expand_more

No. Open-cell foam is highly vapor permeable (high perm rating) and will absorb and hold liquid water like a sponge. Below grade (basements, crawlspace walls) where bulk water and high vapor drive from damp soil are present, closed-cell foam is mandated. Closed-cell foam acts as a Class II vapor retarder (at appropriate thickness) and rejects liquid water absorption.

How does foam thickness affect the R-value per inch? expand_more

For closed-cell foam, the R-value per inch remains relatively consistent (typically R-6.5 to R-7.0 per inch). However, performance testing relies on the 'Aged R-value' (LTTR) which accounts for the gradual diffusion of blowing agents out of the closed cells over time, replacing them with air. It is critical to design around the aged R-value, not the initial R-value. Additionally, thermal bridging through wood studs (R-1.2 per inch) significantly reduces the 'whole-wall' effective R-value, regardless of the cavity insulation used.
